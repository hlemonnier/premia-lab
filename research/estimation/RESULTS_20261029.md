# Post-GC research: no upgrade from robust or Kalman premium estimation

29 October 2026. Main remains the submission-ready GLS ensemble at `3d668e1877541742fcb9f9a5c665ff22ba2f7c76`. GC remains the stronger research candidate, not a promoted release.

## Executive summary

I tested two frozen alternatives for estimating the same characteristic premia. Both retain GC's risk forecasts, correlation estimator, allocation, costs, sizing and fixed market sleeve. **Neither passes the registered research-shortlist rule.** No parameters were retuned and no additional combination was tested.

The robust-date-weighted estimator R reaches **1.548118363**, versus **1.547386552** for GC: only **+0.000731811**. Its first-half Sharpe falls by **0.035504376**, so the near-flat full-period result conceals a redistribution of performance across periods. The Kalman estimator K scores **1.520568038**, below GC in every stage and below the selected GLS model overall. The numerical maximum is not a reason to replace GC with R.

Both new implementations pass the executed software checks. This is negative economic evidence for these two specifications, not a claim that all robust estimators or state-space models fail, or that no further improvement is possible. There was no main update or external submission.

## R: reduce the influence of unusually large premium innovations

R retains the existing pooled GLS likelihood, lagged covariance weights, EW1008/252 memories, coefficient-wise ridge 0.1 and 50/50 F2 combination. It changes one mechanism: a bounded reliability weight applied to each matured training date.

For label u, the projected GLS likelihood is formed using features at u-1, covariance through u-1 and F's variance multiplier forecast for u. The cross-sectional premium estimate is compared with the coefficient estimate available before label u was assimilated. A Student-t-inspired weight with fixed degrees of freedom 5, clipped to [0.05,1], limits large Mahalanobis innovations. It scales that day's Gram matrix and return moment together. It is not an exact Student-t filter or a fitted distributional claim about the hidden generator.

The mean reliability weight on scored dates is **0.91209**. About **30.86%** of dates are downweighted, the minimum is **0.07184**, and the 0.05 floor never binds. Mean weights by stage are 0.92439 / 0.86554 / 0.94551. These are descriptive diagnostics, not grounds for tuning the degrees of freedom or floor after the fact.

The software control with every reliability weight forced to one reproduces original GC's forecasts, positions and economic rows exactly on the constructed E2E workflow. This checks that the new likelihood plumbing has not silently changed the original estimator.

R improves mean daily cross-sectional IC slightly, from **0.015391435 to 0.015456271**, and reduces global forecast MSE from **0.000357913287 to 0.000357910337**. Those tiny improvements do not establish a worthwhile portfolio improvement. Its annual net mean falls from GC's **6.21300% to 5.86926%**, while volatility falls from **4.01516% to 3.79122%**; fees, gross exposure and beta also fall. The first-half deterioration remains. Because its no-sleeve test was not admitted, I do not claim the tiny full-period gain is independent of the explicit market position.

## K: allow the premium estimate to carry its own uncertainty

K replaces only the GLS half of the ensemble with a 27-state Gaussian random-walk filter. Each matured cross-section supplies the same projected precision and information vector as GLS. Posterior covariance and the previous posterior mean determine how much the next observation changes the coefficient estimate. There is no backward smoothing, future likelihood optimization or process-noise search.

The process-noise calibration was fixed before results. The measurement-noise proxy is the eigen-pseudoinverse of the lagged likelihood precision. An EW1008 mean of its diagonal is combined with scalar steady-state gains corresponding to half-lives 504 for linear coefficients and 1008 for squares/beta. The 504-day horizon is the geometric mean of the existing 252/1008 linear memories. If k is that scalar gain and C the noise proxy, q=C*k^2/(1-k). This scalar identity motivates the calibration; it does not guarantee optimality in the full multivariate, misspecified model.

K predicts larger signals than GC on average, increases gross exposure and volatility, and worsens both IC and MSE. Mean IC is **0.015224936**, MSE **0.000357922510**. The annual net mean rises to **6.43969%**, but volatility rises to **4.23506%**, reducing Sharpe. K differs in state dynamics and regularization as well as filtering mechanics: this experiment cannot identify which assumption is responsible for its weaker performance. It rejects this fixed specification, not the entire class of adaptive models.

All intervals at the other registered lengths also include zero. This does not mean the models are equal, nor does a positive bootstrap fraction estimate future success probability. Holm adjustment covers this two-candidate round only, not the entire adaptive research history. There is no fresh holdout and no evidence here supporting promotion of either candidate.

## Software verification and timing

There are **248 passing named workflow assertions**: 82 constructed-data checks for R, 74 for K and 46 supplied-data checks for each. These are software assertions, not independent market experiments. Coverage includes short/zero inputs, training chronology, unchanged GC risk channels, prefixes, future-return/characteristic/joint mutations, session reuse, memory layouts, stock permutation and separate cashflow reconstruction. Every causal/prefix/reset/layout comparison of earlier forecasts or positions has error **0.0**. Mutations change later predictions, so the perturbations have an effect.

Permutation weights use the existing 1e-8 component tolerance; the new aggregate gross bound was declared before implementation as N*1e-8, derived from the component bound. At 48 stocks, observed gross differences are 2.86e-10 for R and 6.23e-10 for K. The old 1e-10 gross-equality assertion would still fail. Earlier failed receipts are not rewritten or relabeled. Forecast, return and fee tolerances stay 1e-10; no scored solver or post-result tolerance was changed.

All four complete normal-fee replays have 7,054 allocator certificates with status `ok`. Separate cashflow errors are below 1e-17. The identity control checks the original GLS path; no independent dense Kalman implementation or independent person/model review was run.

Complete forecast time was **37.52 s for R** and **53.98 s for K**; mean sequential rebalance was **19.66 ms / 23.54 ms**. These are one-thread-per-process measurements on a shared host, with overlapping research jobs. Century forecasts are projections only; no new operational endurance test is claimed. Environment: Python 3.13.5, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.17.0, pandas 2.2.3 and scikit-learn 1.8.0.
