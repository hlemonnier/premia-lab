scorer's development net Sharpe from **1.4447333005566665 to
1.4576547036224103**: **+0.0129214030657441**. It passes the registered development

## What R1 changes

The existing F2 expected-return forecast is unchanged. So are V3's factor-risk
component, certified optimizer, cost horizon, volatility-sizing rule, and fixed
0.05 equal-weight market sleeve. Only the specific-variance input changes.

A `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` with Poisson loss predicts next-day squared
characteristic-residual return divided by prior specific variance. Inputs are
13 standardized published characteristics, standardized trailing beta, and log
prior specific variance. Fixed settings: learning rate 0.05, 80 iterations,
15 leaves, minimum leaf 200, L2 10, seed 20261029, early stopping disabled.
Refits occur every 126 days, using every fifth available day of the previous
504 days. Squared-residual ratios are capped at 25; predicted ratios are clipped
to [0.5,2]. The final variance multiplier is 0.5 + 0.5 times that prediction,
hence bounded to [0.75,1.5]. Both virtual and traded stock books use it.

Forecast row t uses source rows strictly before t. A 506-day ring buffer keeps
the training history bounded; the returned forecast tensor has two channels,
alpha and risk multiplier. The assignment explicitly permits such tensors.
Both channels are included in the causality tests.

Observed full-sample annual mean net return falls from 6.60895% to 6.55856%,
while annual volatility falls from 4.57451% to 4.49939%. This is a small change in
portfolio allocation/risk, not evidence of newly discovered expected returns.
Beta decreases slightly; there is no increase in the market sleeve.

## Uncertainty and negative evidence

| Stage | V3 net Sharpe | R1 net Sharpe | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|

| Second | 1.840144 | 1.853270 | +0.013127 |
| Third | 1.654664 | 1.633865 | -0.020799 |

The fixed gate required full gain >=0.005, improvement in at least two stages,
no stage decline greater than 0.10, absolute full beta <0.18, valid outputs,
causality and runtime. R1 passes, but its final stage is worse. Its second-half
Sharpe also slips slightly (1.759446 versus 1.761294).

A paired circular 63-day block bootstrap, 3,000 draws and seed 20261029, gives a
95% interval for the Sharpe change of **[-0.0074093, +0.0340631]**. The change is
positive in 89.2% of bootstrap draws; that is NOT an 89.2% probability of future
outperformance. The interval includes zero and is not corrected for the overall
research search or choosing the best variant.

More importantly, unweighted next-day residual-variance QLIKE gets WORSE:
mean difference **+0.0089924**, where lower is better. Therefore the portfolio
score gain must not be described as universally better variance prediction.
It may reflect helpful changes in selected exposures, regularization, sampling
noise, or a combination. This wave does not distinguish those explanations.
A matched non-ML variance-reweighting ablation and an independent fresh-clone
replay remain useful unexecuted checks; neither is claimed passed.

## RL: what was and was not tested

Cmix is a causal full-information expert controller, not deep reinforcement
learning. It combines three independently replayed execution policies with
cost horizons 5,20,80, based on EW252 realized net mean/variance after 252
completed observations. It updates every five days and retains 50% baseline
weight. Actual mixed-book drift and trading fees are charged by the original
scorer; expert net returns are not simply averaged. It scores 1.437484.

PPO, SAC, high-dimensional neural portfolio policies, and alternative RL reward
functions were not implemented or tested. This negative control is not evidence
that every RL method fails. The successful test here changes the ML target to
specific risk rather than adding a larger expected-return network.
