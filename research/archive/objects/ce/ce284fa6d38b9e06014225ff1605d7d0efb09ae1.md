# Characteristic-peer relationships: no incremental net-Sharpe gain

## Decision

I tested whether similarity between stocks adds useful predictive information or improves specific-risk estimation beyond the selected GLS ensemble. Neither primary candidate improves net Sharpe, and neither beats its matched non-relational control. I reject both specifications under the plan fixed before implementation.

The selected GLS reference G reproduces **1.5332155606881952** exactly. The previous GC research candidate remains at its archived **1.5473865516722796**; it was not rerun or promoted in this wave. I did not adjust neighbor count, signal horizons, ridge, caps or shrinkage after seeing these results.

## What the data links mean here

I built a graph from each stock's 32 nearest other stocks in the 13 standardized characteristic coordinates. It updates every21 source days using published characteristics only, excludes self, and includes all boundary ties. The names of the characteristics receive no invented economic meaning. This is a statistical similarity graph, not an observed industry/supply-chain network.

For P, I remove the same-day market and characteristic-factor shocks from observed returns, smooth each stock's residual at half-lives5/21, and construct four predictors: its own two smoothed residuals and the corresponding peer averages. I standardize them and project them off the current27-column design, then fit a four-coefficient EW1008 ridge to the next day's residual minus the strictly causal GLS prediction. The newest response always predates the forecast row. The additional prediction is shrunk50% and capped at20% of the current GLS alpha RMS. Risk and allocation remain unchanged. O uses only the two own-stock predictors with the same learning and trading machinery.

For V, I leave the complete GLS forecast and factor risk unchanged. Within both stock books, I blend specific variance50/50 with the neighbor mean, bounding the variance multiplier to[0.75,1.5]. VG uses the whole-universe mean rather than characteristic neighbors. Neither model estimates peer residual covariance or uses a graph neural network.

## Why the prediction idea failed after costs

Mean daily cross-sectional IC increases from **0.015391435 for G to0.016037143 for P**. But the own-only control already reaches **0.016009027**. Most of the measured predictive increment is therefore present without peer links. P's matched net-Sharpe contrast against O is **-0.006747565**.

The additional dynamics induce much more trading: P's annual fees are **1.0764% rather than0.2312%**, about4.66 times G's. Its annual net mean rises slightly (6.4616% versus6.3240%), but volatility rises to4.8180% from4.1247%. Gross Sharpe also declines. The failure is not exclusively fees, and a slightly better IC does not establish a better tradable portfolio.

## Risk and relationship diagnostics

V does not beat global pooling: its net-Sharpe difference against VG is **-0.001302705**. Relative to the existing specific-risk estimate, the squared-residual QLIKE changes are **+0.010232222 for V** and **+0.010491369 for VG**; lower is better, so both worsen this proxy. The squared residual is estimated from the existing factor model, not the hidden generator's true variance.

A descriptive measure of contemporaneous peer co-movement is the mean over scored dates of `v' W v / (v' v)`, where W is the graph available before that return. It is **0.153218278** for market-residual returns and **0.006870939** after removing the characteristic-factor component. These are normalized cross-sectional covariance ratios, NOT time-series Pearson correlations. Their denominators differ, so this is not a percentage-explained calculation or a causal attribution. It supports the interpretation that this graph largely reflects shared exposures already represented by the factor model; it does not rule out all remaining links.

The daily cross-sectional correlation between a stock's specific-variance estimate and its neighbors' mean estimate averages only **0.016451242**. In this particular metric, similar characteristic exposures do not imply similar idiosyncratic variances. That is consistent with the failure of peer-specific-risk smoothing, not proof that all characteristic-conditioned risk models fail.

These diagnostics were explanatory and did not trigger further tuned specifications. Predictive and risk connections are different hypotheses: sharing contemporaneous factor shocks is not evidence of exploitable lead-lag returns.

## Interpretation and next research direction

I would not replace these simple graph tests with a larger neural model on the assumption that capacity will fix them. The current results favor testing whether any stable directed relationship survives the existing factor model and costs before fitting a flexible network. A graph estimated from returns rather than characteristic proximity is a different, untested hypothesis—not an implied success. The selected GLS and the previous GC candidate remain the useful benchmarks.

Methodological motivation only: Avramov/Ge/Li/Linton, *Dual peer effects and cross-stock predictability*, JFE2026, DOI10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104274, publisher/author-repository abstract and introduction; Fan/Liao/Wang, *Projected principal component analysis in factor models*, arXiv1406.3836, abstract. No reported performance from those papers was assumed to transfer here, and these experiments are not replications.
