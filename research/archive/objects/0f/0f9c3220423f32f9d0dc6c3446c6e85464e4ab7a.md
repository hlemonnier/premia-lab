## F changes factor variances, not the alpha forecast

V3 models risk as a diagonal specific-risk component plus a low-rank characteristic-factor covariance. F leaves the specific variances unchanged and predicts a bounded correction to the marginal variance of each of the 26 characteristic factors. [S2,S3]

After source day s closes, the same causal cross-sectional regression as V3 produces factor returns f_s. For each factor, the features are the logarithms of its squared-return EW estimates at half-lives 5, 21 and 63 days, divided by its baseline EW126 second moment and clipped to [0.25,4]. The target is the next day's squared estimated factor return divided by the baseline variance available before that day.

One shared Gamma GLM, with three coefficients and an intercept, pools all 26 factors. It is refitted every 126 days using every fifth matured date in the preceding 504 days, with labels beginning at source row 64. Fixed settings are `alpha=1`, `max_iter=300`, `tol=1e-8`, and a log link. There is no factor-ID dummy, random cross-validation, validation-based early stopping, or hyperparameter search. Factor observations are not treated as independent dates for inference: the uncertainty calculations resample entire daily portfolio observations. [S2,S3,S8]

Predicted variance ratios are clipped to [0.5,2] and blended 50/50 with one, giving multipliers v in [0.75,1.5]. If the old factor covariance is F_old=L_old L_old', the allocation uses:

    A = diag(sqrt(v))
    L_new = A L_old
    F_new = A F_old A

This preserves positive semidefiniteness and V3's contemporaneous factor-correlation estimates. It does not fit a new DCC correlation process. Both the virtual sizing book and the traded stock book use this factor-risk correction. The 0.3 factor-risk scale, F2 alpha learner, optimizer, transaction-cost horizon, limits and 0.05 equal-weight market sleeve are unchanged. Every saved alpha forecast matches B0 exactly (maximum absolute error 0.0). [S2–S4]

## What the controls say

C learns one common specific-risk multiplier from three aggregate multihorizon features, using one observation per selected date. It reaches 1.45710 and its residual-square QLIKE is slightly worse than B0 (+0.000402138). Removing the predictors and retaining only the mean of the same past labels produces I at 1.45937, with worse QLIKE (+0.013524494). These outcomes do not justify adding capacity to the common-specific-risk predictor. [S3]

T uses exactly C's multiplier but applies it to BOTH specific and factor risk. Its score rises to 1.49091, while C stays at 1.45710. This supports the interpretation that WHERE the correction enters the risk matrix matters substantially: changing specific risk alone is not equivalent to scaling total risk. It does not prove that F's factor-by-factor correction adds meaningful economic value beyond T's common scaling. F has better factor-variance proxy loss, but their historical portfolio Sharpes are close. No extra post-hoc model was fit to exploit this observation. [S3]

## Literature used and its limits

Corsi's HAR-RV paper motivates parsimonious multihorizon volatility information. Our exponential-window Gamma model on daily estimated factor returns is NOT a replication of HAR-RV on intraday realized volatility. [S5]

Engle and Sheppard motivate separating marginal variance forecasting from correlation estimation. Here the old correlation estimates are retained; no DCC model is fitted. [S6]

Cederburg and coauthors provide an important counterweight: historical volatility-management gains need not survive real-time implementation or structural change. We therefore use causal fitted rules, retain losing controls and do not interpret in-sample improvements as future guarantees. Accessible published abstracts/metadata and official documentation informed the hypotheses; no published table or numerical result was reproduced. [S7,S8]
